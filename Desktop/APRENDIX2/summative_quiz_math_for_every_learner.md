## QUESTION BANK

### Q1 & Q2: Recall Questions

* Which of the following is an example of a math process skill?
  * Options: Counting to 100 / Measuring length with a ruler / Using logic to justify your thinking / Identifying shapes in the environment

* According to the course, allowing children to talk through math problems in their home language undermines the goal of math instruction.
  * Options: True / False

* Which of the following best describes "number sense"?
  * Options: The ability to add and subtract quickly / Understanding what numbers are and how they relate to each other / Knowing how to use a ruler / Recognizing geometric shapes and their properties

### Q3 & Q4: Understanding Questions

* The course says teachers should "normalize" mistakes in math. What does this look like in your classroom?
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: modeling mistakes as a teacher, celebrating different attempts, reframing errors as learning opportunities, reducing math anxiety, building resilience
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): model, celebrate, reframe, error, mistake, anxiety, resilience, learning, safe*

* What makes a child who can reason mathematically different from a child who has only memorized math facts?
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: reasoning as applying logic and justifying thinking, memorization as recall without understanding, process skills vs rote knowledge, encouraging children to explain their thinking
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): reasoning, logic, justify, explain, understand, apply, process, thinking*

* The course describes several ways to assess children's math understanding without using written tests. Describe one of these approaches.
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: exit pebbles, walk-abouts, show me checks, demonstration prompts, class estimation, hands-on or gesture-based responses
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): observe, check, gesture, show, response, whole class, formative, slate, exit*

### Q5 & Q6: Application Questions

* You notice that several children in your class shut down or go quiet as soon as you introduce a new math topic. They avoid trying problems and wait for you to give the answer. Based on what you learned in this course, what would you do, and why do you think it would help?
  * Evaluated on: identifying the problem as math anxiety or low confidence, drawing on mindset strategies (normalizing mistakes, inclusive language, growth-oriented feedback, choice and voice), explaining why the chosen approach addresses the root cause
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): anxiety, confidence, mistake, normalize, growth, safe, choice, voice, feedback*

* You want your students to understand place value, but you have no printed materials or manipulatives. Describe how you would teach this concept using what is available in or around your classroom and why.
  * Evaluated on: using hands-on strategies (bundling, local materials, number lines, place value charts), connecting concrete materials to abstract concepts
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): bundle, local, stone, stick, cap, concrete, number line, place value, group, chart*

* During a math lesson, you ask a question and only a few children raise their hands. You are not sure whether the others understand or are simply hesitant to participate. What would you do next, and why?
  * Evaluated on: using whole-class response strategies (show me checks, fingers, slates, gestures), walk-abouts, low-stress assessment approaches
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): whole class, show me, gesture, slate, fingers, walk, check, observe, all, participate*

### Q8: Best Practice Question

* Your colleague Amara teaches a multi-grade class and tells you: "I feel like I'm always just teaching to the middle. The children who are ahead are bored, and the children who are behind are lost, and I don't know how to reach everyone at once." Based on what you learned in this course, what advice would you give her?
  * Evaluated on: using inclusive math strategies (multiple ways to respond, visuals and gestures, varied assessment, peer support, choice and voice), reasoning about why differentiation doesn't require different lesson plans, practical and grounded advice
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): multiple, ways, respond, gesture, visual, peer, choice, voice, include, every child*

* Your colleague David says: "My students don't seem to enjoy math at all. They see it as something to tolerate, not something useful." What would you tell him, based on what you learned in this course?
  * Evaluated on: using mindset and relevance strategies (real-world problems, local examples, celebrating multiple strategies, community routines, normalizing mistakes), reasoning about the connection between relevance and motivation, specific and actionable advice
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): real-world, local, relevant, context, mistake, celebrate, motivation, meaning, familiar*

* A newer teacher in your school asks you: "I want my students to really understand math, not just memorize facts. But I don't know where to start because I don't have textbooks or materials." What would you tell her?
  * Evaluated on: using hands-on math strategies (local materials, number cards, number lines, story problems, concrete models), process skills instruction (show-draw-tell, justifying answers, sentence frames), reasoning about the difference between procedural and conceptual understanding, reassurance that low-resource does not mean low quality
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): local, concrete, hands-on, materials, number line, story, justify, explain, understand, process*

**Post-scoring feedback (after all 8 questions, before any retake):**
- Affirm effort first: "You worked hard through this — let's look at how you did."
- For each missed item: name the concept area and give a 1-sentence explanation of the correct idea.
- Never use "wrong" or "incorrect" — use "not quite" or "let's look at that one again."
- Do NOT share model answers or sample responses. Explain the concept, not the answer.

### 4-Question Shortened Retry Assessment

> Used only when first attempt score is 50–79%. Select new items from the bank — not the same items used in the first attempt.

| Slot | Type |
|------|------|
| Q1 | Recall (MC or T/F) |
| Q2 | Understanding (open-ended) |
| Q3–Q4 | Application (scenario-based) |

- Retry pass threshold: ≥80% (3 of 4 correct) → pass → deliver **Completion Message**
- If retry <80% → proceed to **Course Review**

### Course Review

- **Trigger:** Summative score <50% on first attempt, OR retry score <80% after a 50–79% first attempt.
- **Format:** Targeted review (~10 minutes) generated by the bot based on the specific questions the teacher got wrong. Content drawn from module files. Follows `steady_path` delivery.
- **After Review:** Offer the full 8-question summative assessment again (new item bank). Same scoring rules apply. No limit on review cycles.
- **Framing:** Warm and forward-looking — "Let's revisit a few things before you try again." Not a punishment.
