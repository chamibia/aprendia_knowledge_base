### 2. Learning Objectives (2–3)

* Teachers use storytelling and drama to make learning more engaging.

* Teachers use creative expression to include all learners.

* Teachers support students to create, share, and express their own ideas.

### 3. Teacher Motivations & Pain Points

* "My students only think of learning as sitting and listening."

* "When I ask questions, only a few students respond."

* "With no resources, I find it hard to do creative lessons."

* "I want students to participate more, but I don't know how to support them."

### 4. Module Rules

* Emphasize that drama, role play, and creative expression are appropriate for any subject.
* Offer varying examples across school subjects.
* Use local resources and easy-to-collect materials.

### 5. Key Concepts

* **AIL_M5_CON1 / Stories Make Ideas Clear.** Long explanations can be difficult for students to understand. A simple story helps children follow ideas step by step and remember them. Using stories that are familiar and relevant to students' lives makes learning easier to understand and less stressful.

* **AIL_M5_CON2 / Expression Helps Every Child Participate.** Not all students are confident in writing or answering questions out loud. Speaking, acting, or drawing gives more ways to show understanding. This helps include quieter children and those with different learning levels, learning styles, and language backgrounds. Drama, role play, and creativity can be used in any subject and serve as approachable ways for students to interact with content in subjects they may feel less comfortable with.

* **AIL_M5_CON3 / Creating Builds Deeper Understanding.** When children create their own stories or act out ideas, they are thinking, not just repeating or memorizing. This helps them connect new learning to their own experiences. Creation also allows children to access content at their own level while interacting with it in a meaningful way. Creating is not limited to one subject, but can be used across all content areas and all grade levels.

* **AIL_M5_CON4 / Safe Sharing Builds Belonging.** Sharing ideas in front of an audience can be intimidating. When classroom expectations build a positive, supportive learning environment, students feel safe to share. Children build a stronger sense of belonging when they feel heard and respected in the classroom.

### 6. Strategies

#### AIL_M5_STRAT1 / Strategy 1: Use Storytelling to Teach Content

* **Description:** Teach concepts through simple, structured stories.

* **Expanded Explanation:** In classrooms with many learners and limited materials, long explanations can be hard for students to understand and remember. Turning concepts into simple stories helps children visualize and connect ideas. Teachers can use story structures (e.g. beginning-middle-end, problem and solution, cause and effect) to organize the concepts. Teachers should use familiar, local details to make the stories and concepts feel relevant to students (e.g. local names, places, phrases, languages). Stories reduce distraction, support comprehension and memory, and can be used across all subject areas. When telling stories, teachers can use expressive voices and gestures to make the lesson lively and engaging. Teachers can add details to make a story interesting (e.g. "Grandpa Amadou loved cows. Even when he was just a small boy, he dreamed of being a cow herder and having 200 cows." rather than "Grandpa Amadou was a cow herder."). The story should always connect to the content, making a concept easier to understand rather than going off topic.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Teacher invites a student to help with the storytelling for a new vocabulary word.

  * Teacher asks students to retell the story to a partner.

  * Teacher asks questions during the storytelling (e.g. "What do you think will happen next?").

  * Teacher uses a beginning-middle-end structure to tell a story related to division (e.g. "Fatou sold bracelets every week at the market, but this week she wanted to make bracelets for her friends. Fatou worked hard and made 21 bracelets to share with her 5 friends. She drew a circle on the ground for each friend and began placing them inside the circles to share them equally…").

  * Teacher uses a problem-solution structure to tell a story about responsibility (e.g. "Musa goes every evening after school to water his mom's garden. The garden is so beautiful and green; it is filled with vegetables for the family to eat. Musa goes to the tap, but he forgot his bucket at home! The sun is hot and Musa does not want to walk home and back again. Musa considers not watering the plants, but remembers the word responsibility…").

  * Teacher uses a cause-and-effect structure to tell a story about ending a sentence with punctuation (e.g. "Amie is writing a note to her older sister. She wants to impress her with her writing skills. Amie uses a capital letter, spaces, and her best handwriting. Then she spins her finger in the air and writes a period at the end of the sentence. When she gives the note to her sister, she is so happy…").

* **Reflection Prompt:** What concept could you turn into a story featuring a local place this week?

* **Quiz Item:** What is one benefit of using storytelling to teach? (Options: It makes the lesson longer / It replaces learning / It helps students understand and remember)

* **Teacher Voice:** "My students remember the story better than the explanation."

#### AIL_M5_STRAT2 / Strategy 2: Let Children Create and Tell Their Own Stories

* **Description:** Invite students to create their own examples, characters, or scenarios about what they are learning.

* **Expanded Explanation:** When children create their own stories, they actively connect new ideas to their experiences. Allowing students to be authors helps them see their ideas and thinking as valuable. Teachers can encourage students to create stories that can be shared aloud, written, drawn, or acted out. In large classes, it can be difficult for every student to share their story — teachers can set the expectation that not every story will be shared in one session, but ensure students that everyone will eventually be given an opportunity. When children create their own stories, they are using higher order thinking skills. Their brains are working hard to creatively connect to the content and build confidence. Teachers can use student storytelling across all subject areas. Allowing students to be creative can also support engagement in subjects or topics that they are struggling with.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Students create a story using new vocabulary words and act it out for the class.

  * Teacher gives a math problem (e.g. 8×3=) and students draw a story to represent the problem and answer.

  * Students are given a moral (e.g. a friend shares, respect elders, words can hurt or help others, be kind always) and they create or draw a story that represents it.

  * Students create a story and share it with a partner, not the whole class.

* **Reflection Prompt:** How can you give students more opportunities to create their own stories?

* **Quiz Item:** Why does creating their own stories help children learn more deeply? (Keywords: connect ideas, ownership, thinking, engagement)

* **Teacher Voice:** "I was surprised how creative my students were."

#### AIL_M5_STRAT3 / Strategy 3: Use Drama for Perspective-Taking

* **Description:** Use role play to help children explore different viewpoints and understand ideas more deeply.

* **Expanded Explanation:** Drama allows children to step into different roles and see situations from different perspectives. It can also help students understand ideas that are difficult to explain (e.g. the process of setting up a stall at the market; respectful ways to use public transportation). Drama builds empathy, critical thinking, and understanding. It also provides a low-pressure way for children to participate, especially for students who are not yet confident in their reading or writing abilities. Role play allows students to act out ideas using their bodies and voices, which is helpful for classrooms with limited resources. Drama can keep all students engaged at once, either as actors or as viewers.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Students play different roles on a public bus (e.g. bus driver, mother with an infant, student going to school) and discuss what using public transport respectfully could look like.

  * Small groups are given a topic and create a drama with different characters.

  * Two groups act out the same scenario with different character reactions (e.g. a seller will not negotiate the price and the buyer gets upset; then the seller will not negotiate and the buyer thanks them anyway and walks away calmly).

  * After sharing a text, the teacher asks students to act like one of the characters responding to a situation (e.g. "How would this character respond to losing the football match?").

* **Reflection Prompt:** What roles could students act out in your next lesson?

* **Quiz Item:**

  * What does drama help children practice? (Options: Perspective-taking / Sitting quietly / Copying only)

  * How does role play help children understand different perspectives? (Keywords: act, experience, viewpoints, empathy, roles)

* **Teacher Voice:** "Even quiet students joined when we acted it out."

#### AIL_M5_STRAT4 / Strategy 4: Create an Audience

* **Description:** Give students the opportunity to share their work with others.

* **Expanded Explanation:** When students know they will be sharing their ideas, the quality of their thinking and preparation increases. Sharing builds confidence and helps children see themselves as capable learners. It also creates a sense of community and mutual respect. Audiences can be as simple as other students in the class, or can include other classes, teachers, or caregivers. Teachers can use simple structures like pair performances, gallery walks, and group presentations. When teachers create classroom experiences where students can identify as authors, performers, and creators, students see learning as active and celebratory.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Class takes a gallery walk to view each student's drawing of a scene to depict the history lesson.

  * Pairs of students create a song for the water cycle and sing it for the class.

  * Teacher asks students to think of a person from the shared text they would like to eat lunch with. Students draw the lunch and write two questions they would like to ask the character, then share in a class circle.

  * Small groups create a mini-text to read and act out. The groups practice, perform for the class, and then perform for another class at the school.

* **Reflection Prompt:** How can you create simple opportunities for students to share their work?

* **Quiz Item:**

  * Creating audiences for student work helps students build confidence. (Options: True / False)

  * Why does sharing work with others increase student effort? (Keywords: motivation, pride, preparation, seen)

* **Teacher Voice:** "My students take pride when others see their work."
