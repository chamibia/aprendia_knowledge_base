### 2. Learning Objectives (2–3)

* Teachers use reading and writing to build joy, pride, and connection in the classroom.
* Teachers design interactive literacy experiences that center student voices and identities.
* Teachers create opportunities for students to see themselves as readers and authors.

### 3. Module Rules

* Focus on joy, identity, healing, and connection instead of technical decoding instruction
* Prioritize participation over correctness
* Emphasize shared learning over individual evaluation
* Do not favor one language or culture represented in the classroom

### 4. Key Concepts

* **READING_M5_CON1 / Reading as hope.** Stories can offer comfort, hope, and new possibilities. Through characters who solve problems, show kindness, forgive others, or overcome challenges, children can safely process emotions and imagine positive outcomes. In crisis-affected communities, reading is not only academic, but also emotional support. Reading can act as a safe and predictable activity that offers rest, ignites curiosity, and instills hope.

* **READING_M5_CON2 / Identity builds motivation**. Children engage more deeply when they see their names, languages, cultures, and communities represented in stories. Familiarity makes reading feel relevant and meaningful instead of distant. When children feel seen, they build motivation and pride. Many children learn to read in a language that is not the one they speak at home. When teachers welcome different languages and cultures in the classroom, students will feel belonging and want to engage in classroom learning.

* **READING_M5_CON3 / Participation deepens meaning.** Reading is not only speaking or listening; reading is thinking, questioning, and imagining. When students are active participants in the reading and storytelling process, they can develop deeper, more meaningful ideas. Reading experiences that are joyful and fun allow students to develop curiosity and be engaged throughout learning. When students are excited to participate, they will grow as learners.

* **READING_M5_CON4 / Creativity strengthens ownership**. Creating stories, retelling events, and drawing scenes allows students to learn that their ideas matter. In crisis-affected contexts, children may have limited control over their lives, but opportunities to create can restore a child's sense of voice and agency. When students create, they experience ownership and understand they have a valuable role in the classroom. Creation allows students to see themselves as more than students, but also readers, writers, authors, and contributors.

### 5. Strategies

#### READING_M5_STRAT1 / Strategy 1: Making Reading Aloud Expressive and Interactive

* **Description:** Read stories with expression, gestures, and simple questions to invite participation
* **Expanded Explanation:** When teachers change their voice, use facial expression, or add small action, stories come alive. Teachers should review stories before reading them to their class to practice where to use voices or gestures. Teachers can also prepare simple questions to ask during different parts of the story to engage students (e.g. "What do you think will happen next?"). Expressive reading allows students to connect to the text in an exciting and engaging way. In crowded classrooms, interactive reading reduces restlessness and helps children connect to not only, but each other. Expressions can be memorable for students and help them engage in discussion about the reading later (e.g. "Remember during the read aloud when our teacher made wind noises, this made me think of…"). 
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher asks a variety of questions before, during, and after reading.
  * Teacher uses hand movements for specific words (e.g. "big farm" = open arms wide, "rain" = wiggle fingers while moving hand down)
  * Teacher invites students to act out gestures and small movements (e.g. "Our character is dancing again, lets all do a little dance in our seats." )
  * Students turn to a partner and whisper their prediction (e.g. "Oh no! Our character is late for school, turn and whisper to a friend what you think will happen next.")
  * Teacher uses different voices for different characters (e.g. deep, squeaky, soft, small, whisper, loud)
  * Class says repeated words together (e.g. The story is about saying 'thank you." Anytime a character is supposed to say 'thank you' in the story, the teacher points to the class and they say "thank you.")
* **Reflection Prompt:** What makes students engaged when I'm reading aloud?
* **Quiz Item:** Why does expressive reading help children learn? (Keywords: engaged, active learning, fun, movement)
* **Teacher Voice:** "When I use different voices, even my quiet students lean forward."

#### READING_M5_STRAT2 / Strategy 2: Create a Class Story Together

* **Description:** Tell a story as a whole class to build shared ownership and language skills
* **Expanded Explanation:** When children help create a story, they feel ownership and pride. Including students' ideas, names, and familiar places makes the story feel meaningful and affirming. Writing their ideas on the board shows that their words matter. This strategy creates rich reading material without needing many books. Students can practice creative skills, like brainstorming, and collaborative skills, like combining ideas and compromising. It is important to include various students while building a story to ensure inclusiveness. Creating class stories regularly, allows students to understand that their ideas might not be used now, but they can be for the next story. Teachers should act as facilitators to guide student ideas and collaboration. Teachers should use encouraging language that celebrates student participation and ideas (e.g. "This was a good contribution to the beginning of the story"). If students are not moving through story sequences, teachers should prompt them through sequence words (e.g. "I am hearing a lot of good ideas. What happens next?... What happens in the middle?...What will happen at the end?")
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * When students share long ideas, teacher uses rephrasing to help synthesize for the class (e.g. "Thank you for sharing! I heard you say….. and ……. How could we incorporate that into our story?")
  * Teacher allows students to act out or draw ideas for the story
  * Teacher writes sentence frames on the board to guide student thinking (e.g. "First _____. Then _____. Finally ______.")
  * Teacher writes ideas on the board and have students work in groups to turn them into sentences (either written or spoken, depending on students' levels)
  * Teacher prompts thinking by asking students the setting and characters (e.g. "Should our story take place in the village? The market?" "Amie and Mariama are our main characters? Should someone else in their family be in the story too?")
  * Teacher writes final story on a poster and display it in the classroom
* **Reflection Prompt:** How can I make sure all voices are included?
* **Quiz Item:**
  * When creating a class story, it is important to: (Options: Correct every small mistake/Focus on teacher contributions/Value student ideas)
  * How do class stories make reading relevant? (Keywords: connect real life to reading, student ideas, familiar, ownership)
* **Teacher Voice:** "My students feel proud hearing their class story read aloud."

#### READING_M5_STRAT3 / Strategy 3: Invite Creative Expression

* **Description:** Give students regular opportunities to draw, talk, act, or write about their ideas
* **Expanded Explanation:** Every student has unique abilities and talents they bring to the classroom. Drawing, talking, acting, and writing are all valid ways to share their ideas. Depending on a student's own abilities or interests, some ways to share will feel more engaging and meaningful. This allows each student to participate at their own level in a creative way. Teachers should give students choice during creative classroom activities (e.g. "We are predicting what the next event in the story is. You can draw, write, or talk with a friend to share your idea.") Creative expression allows students to know their ideas matter, building both academic skills and self-worth.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * After a story, teacher asks students to draw or write their favorite part.
  * Students retell the story to a partner in their home language
  * Students work individually or in small groups to create a character for the next class story (e.g. draw the character, act like the character, talk or write about the character) and share with the class
  * Students draw a scene from the story and class does a "gallery walk" around the classroom viewing each other's work
  * Students write how the story connected to their life or experiences
* **Reflection Prompt:** What ways do my students like to share their ideas?
* **Quiz Item:**
  * What is an example of creative expression? (Options: Copy sentences/Practice spelling/Drawing a favorite scene)
  * Creative expression helps students process their emotions. (Options: True/False)
* **Teacher Voice:** "One quiet student shared a powerful story reflection through their writing."

#### READING_M5_STRAT4 / Strategy 4: Choose Questions that Reflect Children's World

* **Description:** Select stories that include and celebrate familiar languages, values, and cultures
* **Expanded Explanation:** When children recognize their world in a story, they feel proud and connected. Familiar settings reduce confusion and help comprehension. In multilingual classrooms, including different home languages builds respect and belonging, strengthening student identity. Teachers should recognize the various cultures and languages represented in their classroom to include all students. Teachers should be cautious and avoid promoting one culture over another. Stories that are reflective of student's real lives can create engaging discussion that the teacher can prompt through simple questioning (e.g. "I see beautiful patterns on the clothing. Is the pattern special?"). Integrating stories that connect events or celebrations that are occurring in communities allows students to make connections between reading and real-life.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher tells a story about a local celebration, tradition, or daily routine
  * Character names and settings are from the community or classroom
  * Teacher adapts a traditional story to reflect current community/student life
  * Teacher invites students to learn a story a traditional story at home and retell it to the class
  * If a text is not reflective of students' communities, teacher invites the students to collaborate and change the story to reflect local problems and solutions
* **Reflection Prompt:** Do I use stories that reflect the real lives of my students?
* **Quiz Item:**
  * How might students feel when stories reflect their world? (Keywords: proud, seen, respected, excited)
  * When adapting a story, you can: (Options: Give characters local names/Avoid using familiar language/Only change the setting)
* **Teacher Voice:** "My students laughed when they recognized the local market in the story."

#### READING_M5_STRAT5 / Strategy 5: Reader's Theater

* **Description:** Students work together to perform a reading of a text or story.
* **Expanded Explanation:** Reader's Theater allows students to practice reading a text multiple times with a purpose- perform it for their class. Teacher breaks students into small groups (4 to 6 students) and provides short, simple scripts. They should include enough characters so each student has a role. Scripts can reflect students' daily lives (e.g. going to school, helping a friend, watching a football match), making the reading meaningful. Reader's Theater does not require special materials, only teacher-written scripts. The scripts should contain decodable sentences, with dialogue that forms a simple, clear story. In low resource classrooms, students can copy the script into their notebooks or share the teacher's text. Students will practice performing their script together, helping one another decode the sentences for their character. Then students will perform for the class, reading from their scripts to focus on accuracy, smoothness, and expression. Performing in front of peers builds confidence and even hesitant readers may be willing to participate when they are part of a group. Teachers should review that when the class is the audience, they should be respectful, kind, and encouraging. This is an activity that allows students to be creative and have fun with reading.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher uses known vocabulary words in the scripts
  * Students create their own short scripts using familiar sentence structures
  * Teacher uses the past weekly text studies as themes for the scripts
  * Students create small props to use during their performance
  * Students practice and perform for their class, then perform for other classes in the school
* **Reflection Prompt:** How might performing a text for classmates change the way students practice reading?
* **Quiz Item:**
  * Why do students read from a script instead of memorizing? (Keywords: focus on decoding, expression, develop reading skills)
  * During practice, students improve fluency skills by: (Options: memorizing the script/skipping difficult words/rereading their lines)
* **Teacher Voice:** "When I introduced Reader's Theater, I noticed students reading with more expression and excitement."
