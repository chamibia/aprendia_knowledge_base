### Q3–Q4 Bank: Understanding Questions (Q3 and Q4 are UNDERSTANDING — not Application)

* How can linking words to meaning help students think of themselves as thoughtful readers?
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: prompting class discussion, applying learning to their own lives, making connections to the real world, connecting reading to things they already know or have experienced
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): meaning, connect, real world, thinking, discussion, apply, understand, identity*

* The course suggests several ways using familiar language can help students develop reading skills. Describe one of these approaches.
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: students talk in pairs in a familiar language, create stories in a familiar language, say a new term in a familiar language and in the language of instruction, read stories that use familiar languages and reflect local communities
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): familiar, language, home, pair, discuss, bridge, community, translate, local*

* One of the building blocks of reading is helping children hear the sounds in language. What is one activity you can do in your classroom to support this?
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: clapping the syllables of students' names, isolating beginning sounds, creating sentences with rhyming words, sound games, blending sounds together
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): sound, syllable, clap, rhyme, blend, segment, hear, phoneme, game*

### Q5–Q6 Bank: Application Questions (Q5 and Q6 are BOTH APPLICATION — not Understanding)

* Several students in your class speak different languages at home. You notice these students are quiet during reading activities and do not participate. How could you help these students?
  * Evaluated on: suggesting gestures, visuals, pictures, modeling, and multiple ways to participate; encouraging a safe and supportive environment for speaking and reading; normalizing mistakes; allowing students to choose which language to respond in
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): gesture, visual, multilingual, home language, choice, participate, safe, model, include*

* During independent reading time, you notice that some students are losing their place on the page, skipping lines, or reading the same line twice. They seem to get frustrated and are starting to give up. What would you do to support these students and why?
  * Evaluated on: introducing finger tracking as a tool for directionality and following the text; explaining that physical tracking supports fluency especially for emerging readers; normalizing finger tracking as a helpful reading strategy rather than a sign of weakness
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): finger tracking, directionality, fluency, track, line, place, support, emerging, normalize*

* You are teaching your students to blend sounds into words. Several students say each sound separately, but freeze when trying to pull the sounds together to say the word. How might you help these students and why do you think it would work?
  * Evaluated on: using gestures or manipulatives during blending practice (tapping stones, using a blending mat, sliding finger across arm, chopping motions); connecting physical movement to the act of blending; explaining how concrete approaches support students to see blending as achievable
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): gesture, tap, slide, movement, manipulative, concrete, blend, physical, together*

### Q8: Best Practice Question (Q8 is BEST PRACTICE — not Application, not Recall, not Understanding)

* A new teacher at your school comes to you and says, "I teach new vocabulary by writing the word and definition on the board, and students copy it. But when I test them later, they seem to have forgotten everything." What would you tell them, based on what you learned in this course?
  * Evaluated on: encouraging meaningful instruction over memorization alone; suggesting picture talks, gestures, discussion, or connecting vocabulary to real-life situations; explaining why meaning-based approaches help words stick
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): meaning, connect, picture, gesture, real life, discuss, vocabulary, memorize, context*

* Your colleague Grace tells you, "I read aloud to my students every day, but I am not sure it is actually helping them become better readers. They enjoy it, but I don't see them thinking critically about what we read together or when they read on their own." What advice would you give?
  * Evaluated on: suggesting interactive read-aloud strategies rather than just listening; encouraging discussion, different types of questions, critical thinking activities; allowing students different ways to interact and respond to the stories and texts
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): interactive, question, discuss, respond, think, comprehension, open-ended, critical, participate*

* You and a teacher are chatting during break when they say, "My students have nothing to read. There are no books in our school, no printer, and I don't see how I can teach reading." Based on this course, what advice would you give them?
  * Evaluated on: suggesting local materials to create books, oral storytelling, songs, environmental print; explaining how reading skills are not only developed through physical books; creating reusable resources; writing texts on the board
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): local, oral, environment, sign, board, create, storytelling, reusable, print, material*

## SCORING RUBRICS

Use these rubrics to evaluate responses throughout the full quiz and the shortened retry.

**Q1–Q2 — Recall:** These questions have one correct answer each. Evaluate whether the teacher selected or stated it correctly.

**Q3–Q4 — Understanding (Short Answer):** Evaluate whether the teacher understands a concept from the course — not whether they can recite it. Do not penalize informal or simple language. Focus on whether the response reflects genuine understanding of why or how something works.

**Q5–Q6 — Application (Scenario):** Evaluate whether the teacher can apply and synthesize course concepts to a real classroom situation. Do not penalize informal language or brief responses. Focus on whether the response shows instructional reasoning applied to a realistic teaching scenario.

**Q7 — Observation (Art-Based):** The bot presents an image of a classroom interaction, lesson moment, or teacher-student exchange. The teacher identifies the strategy or strategies they observe and explains what purpose they serve. There is no single correct answer — responses must connect to course content. Evaluate: can the teacher recognize course strategies in practice when they see them?

**Q8 — Best Practice Sharing:** The bot presents a short scenario in which a fictionalized colleague is facing a challenge related to course content and asks the teacher for advice. The teacher responds as they would to a real colleague. Evaluate whether the advice is grounded in course content, appropriate to the colleague's situation, and clearly explained. Evaluate: can the teacher communicate and apply course learning in a peer or mentoring context?

- **Wait for acknowledgment:** After the review, wait for the teacher to acknowledge it (any affirmative reply — "okay," "ready," "got it") before offering next steps.

- **Framing:** Warm and forward-looking, never a punishment — e.g. "Let's go over a few things together, then you can pick whichever path feels right."
