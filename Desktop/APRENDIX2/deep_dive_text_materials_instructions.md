### 2. Learning Objectives (2–3)

* Teachers use community and classroom print as meaningful reading resources.
* Teachers create simple, low-cost reading materials to support hands-on activities.
* Teachers use one text multiple days to build decoding, fluency, and comprehension skills.

### 3. Module Rules

* Emphasize reusing texts as a strength, not a limitation
* Present repetition as repeated interaction with a text, not rote memorization
* Environmental print activities and examples are safe
* Where traditional texts (storybooks, textbooks) are not available, remind the user that they can create or find texts in their environment

### 4. Key Concepts

* **READING_M7_CON1 / Print is Everywhere**. Reading is not limited to books. Signs, labels, clothes, and posters are authentic texts that connect literacy to everyday life. Using environmental print shows students that reading has purpose beyond the classroom. When students view reading as relevant, motivation and participation increases. Seeing print in the environment bridges the gap between classroom reading practice and students' worlds.

* **READING_M7_CON2 / Intentional repetition builds mastery**. Children become confident readers through intentional, repeated exposure to a text. Repetition, in this case, does not mean memorization. Rather, repetition is revisiting a text to practice reading skills like decoding, fluency, and comprehension. Familiar text allows students to focus less on figuring out what the text says and more on how it works. As students continue working with one text, they begin to improve their reading skills and view themselves as capable readers.

* **READING_M7_CON3 / Manipulatives make reading visible**. When children can move letters, build words, and physically arrange sentences, reading becomes concrete and visible. Manipulatives turn abstract print into something students can touch and organize. Hands-on interactions strengthen decoding and fluency skills, as students are able to connect sounds to how words are formed. These interactions are also low-stake ways for students to play with letters, sounds, and words.

* **READING_M7_CON4 / Creation expands the library.** When students create texts, the classroom library grows. Student-made material builds identity and ownership while increasing classroom reading resources. Students who contribute to classroom libraries not only gain experience as readers but also authors. Creating and reading support belonging and confidence as students take shared ownership of the learning environment.

### 5. Strategies

#### READING_M7_STRAT1 / Strategy 1: One Text, Four Ways

* **Description:** Use one text in different ways throughout the school week
* **Expanded Explanation:** One text can support different reading skills, like decoding, fluency, comprehension, and vocabulary. In low-resource classrooms, one text, four ways can serve as a reading text throughout an entire school week, needing limited materials. The texts should be 3 to 5 sentences long and include a title.
  * Day one: The teacher introduces the text through writing the title and drawing pictures that represent the beginning, middle, and end of the story. The teacher asks students open-ended questions, allowing them to make predictions and inferences about the text (e.g. "What do you think will happen in our story?"). The teacher also introduces the key vocabulary words from the text on day one, using movement and gestures to review them.
  * Day two: The teacher writes the text on the board and leads a shared reading using echo and choral strategies. Teachers should listen to hear the words students struggle with during the shared reading. They decode the words that are difficult for students, modeling how to segment and blend the sounds.
  * Day three: The teacher guides the class to reread the text again for fluency and repeat decoding strategies as needed to help students.
  * Day four: The teacher gives the students comprehension tasks. These can be three to five questions that allow the students to think critically about the text (e.g. "Share with me the beginning, middle, and end of the story in three sentences or three pictures." "What was the lesson you learned from the story that you could apply to your own life?"). Teachers can provide students with choice for the comprehension tasks on day four.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teachers use a physical book as the text or writes an original text on a poster or board
  * Teachers add new vocabulary on word cards and display them throughout the school week
  * Students work in pairs to practice rereading the text
  * Class uses different voices to reread the text (e.g. loud, quiet, whisper) and practice fluency
  * Teacher uses texts that reflect the students communities (e.g. familiar names, settings, activities)
* **Reflection Prompt:** How can you make rereading feel purposeful rather than repetitive?
* **Teacher Voice:** "By the end of the week, my struggling readers feel more confident."

#### READING_M7_STRAT2 / Strategy 2: Class Decodable Sentences

* **Description:** Using decoding skills to write simple sentences
* **Expanded Explanation:** Modeling writing skills during reading lessons helps students build confidence as emerging readers and writers. Teachers say a simple sentence aloud ("The dog is on the mat."). Then they model how to stretch out the sounds of each word, or segment and blend to decode unknown words in order to write the sentence. Teachers should model repeating the sentence to give students clarity. When students have to decode multiple words in a sentence the meaning can get lost and saying the sentence again helps students comprehend. As students become more familiar with this model of writing practice, the teacher should introduce new, unfamiliar words (e.g. watermelon, night, date, hear). Teachers should also review how their sentences reflect good writing skills (e.g. capital letter, punctuation, spacing, neat handwriting) and encourage students to think of these skills when they are writing.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher allows students to come up with simple sentences
  * Teacher models gestures (e.g. chopping, clapping) to segment the sounds of unfamiliar words
  * Students write the sentences in a notebook or on a slate
  * Teacher uses new vocabulary words to create sentences, or words that feature new blends or digraphs (e.g. bl, br, gh, ch, ck)
* **Reflection Prompt:** What is a simple sentence you could decode and write with your class?
* **Teacher Voice:** "Reading together as a class made my students comfortable trying to read independently."

#### READING_M7_STRAT3 / Strategy 3: Environmental Print Hunt

* **Description:** Use labels, signs, bags, or packaging as real-world reading practice.
* **Expanded Explanation:** Community print connects literacy to daily life as students read signs, food packaging, or classroom labels. These texts are short, yet meaningful. In low-resource settings environmental print can increase reading opportunities without cost. Teachers can use environmental print as a way to connect reading to real-world experiences by inviting students to bring in examples of environmental print from their home (e.g. wrapper or label). Teachers can post classroom labels around the learning environment using small paper cards (e.g. door, board, desk, pens); this not only acts as print in the classroom, but also supports growing student vocabulary.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher allows students to walk around the classroom and find three examples of environmental print
  * Teacher creates an "environmental print list" (e.g. Students can bring in or share an example of environmental print and the teacher adds the words to a displayed poster, showing all the words the class sees in their everyday lives)
  * Teacher leads class on a community/school walk and students point out different words or letters they see
  * Students create labels for objects in the classroom or their homes
* **Reflection Prompt:** What environmental print exists around my classroom or community?
* **Teacher Voice:** "Students were excited to share the print from their homes."

#### READING_M7_STRAT4 / Strategy 4: Create Letter/Word Cards

* **Description:** Make simple cards for decoding and sentence-building practice
* **Expanded Explanation:** Letter and word cards allow hands-on practice for students to play with sounds and build sentences. This is an active learning strategy that can strengthen many dimensions of reading (print concepts, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension). Cards can be reused during daily phonics practice, small-group instruction, or whole group instruction. Teachers can make a set of letter cards for capital and small letters. Cards should be written as clearly and neatly as possible, as students will model what they see. When making word cards for sentences, teachers can create sentence sets. The sets should have various word cards so that students can make several different sentences. (e.g. I, my, am, have, don't, older, a, younger, sister, brother, is, sibling, tall, short, nice, happy). Sentence sets allow students to play with different words to convey an idea. Importantly, it also makes children ask themselves if a sentence they created makes sense, which is a key comprehension task. Teachers should try to gather durable materials to create the word cards and letters cards on (e.g. cardboard, heavy paper, bottlecaps).
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher allows students to work in small groups with sentence sets, after 5 to 10 minutes the students rotate sentence sets and build sentences with new word cards
  * Teacher use blending squares to help students decode words created by letter cards
  * Teacher creates blend and digraph cards (e.g. bl, sl, fl, dr, tr, st, ch, sh, wh, th) to use for building words (language dependent)
  * Students use letter cards to practice changing the beginning, medial, or final sounds in simple words
  * Students determine if words are real or made-up/nonsense words
  * Use letter cards to create a word in language of instruction and then say it in a familiar language
* **Reflection Prompt:** How can I use letters and word cards to make reading practice more active?
* **Teacher Voice:** "Building words help my students understand how sounds connect."

#### READING_M7_STRAT5 / Strategy 5: Let Children be Authors

* **Description:** Support children in creating simple books using drawings, labels, or short sentences
* **Expanded Explanation:** Early readers and writers can create meaningful texts. Teachers should encourage children to use creativity and imagination to design their own children's books. Teachers can fold paper, or tie cardboard together, to create small books. If teachers plan to allow students to create books, they should ensure to collect enough materials for each student, partners, or small groups to create their own books; having adequate materials will allow every learner to be included and participate. Teachers can create an example simple book to model to students how to structure the book. Teachers should instruct the students to plan their book before giving them the materials to make the final version. Students can brainstorm on a slate, notebook, or orally share their idea with the teachers. Once students have a complete story idea, the teacher can give students the materials to create their book, and students can use only drawings, drawing and writing, or only writing to tell their story. Becoming authors builds student confidence and motivates children to read and write more. This can also provide a classroom, and school, with new reading materials for students.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher provides a structured guide to help develop their book (e.g. "Your book will be five pages, the first will be the book cover where you put the book title. The second page will introduce your main character. The third page…")
  * Teacher creates a "book corner" or "class library" where students made books are stored and available to read
  * Students read their books with a partner, small group, or whole class
  * Students from older grades help students brainstorm, write, and create their books
  * Students only draw pictures to tell a story
  * Students draw pictures then label it with words or a simple sentence
  * Teacher encourages students to use decoding skills to spell words, or use vocabulary words they have learned during reading class
* **Reflection Prompt:** Do my students believe they are capable of writing a book?
* **Teacher Voice:** "Students kept reading their books to each other."
