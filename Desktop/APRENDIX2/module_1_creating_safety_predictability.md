### 2. Learning Objectives (2–3)

* Teachers establish daily routines that help students feel calm and ready to learn.

* Teachers use consistent, calm responses to create a positive classroom environment.

* Teachers collaborate with students to build shared responsibility, trust, and community. 

### 3. Teacher Motivations & Pain Points

* “Some students get scared or angry fast, and I don’t know how to help.”

* “My class is noisy and distracted. I spend more time trying to keep order than teaching.”

* “I don’t want to punish students, but I don’t know what else to do.”

* “When the classroom is chaotic, I feel stressed.”

### 4. Module Rules

- Do not include punitive, violent, or exclusionary discipline examples.  
- Prioritize prevention over reaction to challenging behavior.  
- Reinforce the importance of consistency when establishing routine and how predictability reduces stress.  
- When introducing co-creating classroom rules (Strategy 4), always encourage the teacher to make the rules positive (positive phrasing like “Be a good listener” is more effective than negative phrasing like “Do not speak while others are talking”).

### 5. Key Concepts

* **HC_M1_CON1 / Routines create safety.** Daily routines help children know what to expect during the school day. When the structure of the day is predictable, students feel calmer and more ready to learn. In stressful or unpredictable environments, routines act as known patterns that provide stability and reduce anxiety. 

* **HC_M1_CON2 / Collaboration establishes shared responsibility**. Teachers and students working together to create classroom routines, rules, and expectations allows everyone to share ownership of the learning environment. Shared decision-making helps students understand what is expected of them, reducing uncertainty and fear. This collaboration allows students to become actors in maintaining a positive and safe classroom. 

* **HC_M1_CON3 /Consistency builds trust**. When teachers respond in steady, predictable ways, children learn what to expect. Consistency establishes that rules are fair and not personal. Calm, fair routines and expectations tell children they are protected and valued, allowing a trusting relationship between teacher and students. This trust can reduce unwanted behaviors and promote active, engaged learning. 

* **HC_M1_CON4 / Calm teachers help calm students**. Children learn how to handle stress by watching adults. When teachers stay calm and consistent, children learn to regulate their own emotions. Mindful strategies, like calm voices and neutral body language, are important to model to students. These responses prevent escalation of disruptive behaviors, support a positive learning environment, and support wellbeing for both students and teachers. 

### 6. Strategies

#### **HC_M1_STRAT1 / Strategy 1: Daily Routine & Agenda**

* **Description:** Setting a simple daily schedule helps children know what comes next and feel safe.

* **Expanded Explanation:** Many children feel more secure when they can see and hear what will happen during the day. Sharing the daily routine aloud and posting it visually helps students learn the normal classroom patterns. Throughout the school day, teachers can refer back to the routine, prompting students to share what parts of the agenda the class has done, is doing, and will do next. Predictable patterns are particularly supportive for children who have experienced stress, trauma, or frequent changes. 

* **Examples / Variations:** 

  * Review the daily schedule at the beginning of the school day

  * Have a visual representation of the the routine (written or picture form) and have it posted where all students can see 

  * Use transition phrases and questions “We just finished Math. It is time to begin Writing.” or “Now that library is over, what should we do next? … Yes, we go outside for break.” 

* **Reflection Prompt:** Which part of your day feels least predictable right now?

* **Quiz Item:** Why does a daily routine help students feel calm? (Key words: safety, reduce anxiety/stress, predictability)

* **Teacher Voice:** “When students know what to expect, they are more engaged in class because they feel safe and prepared.”

#### **HC_M1_STRAT2 / Strategy 2: Morning Greeting**

* **Description:** Welcoming students by name makes them feel known and safe.

* **Expanded Explanation:** A greeting builds trust and emotional safety before learning begins. When students are welcomed by name, they feel seen, valued, and known as individuals. Greeting students at the door also allows the teacher to assess students’ moods or signs of distress, helping teachers make informed decisions on how to best support different students. Morning greetings can set a positive tone for the school day. A simple statement like, “I am glad you are here today,” can help students feel safe, valued, and excited to learn. 

* **Examples / Variations:** 

  * Stand at the door for student arrival (and dismissal too)

  * Greet every student by name 

  * Create a “Hello/Goodbye” poster with pictures of different actions (smile, wave, handshake, thumbs up, etc.) for students to choose as a greeting 

* **Reflection Prompt:** Which students would benefit most from a daily greeting?

* **Quiz Item:** When are morning greetings most effective? (Keywords: arrival, morning, before teaching, at the door)

* **Teacher Voice:** “My students feel like they belong when I greet them.”

#### **HC_M1_STRAT3 / Strategy 3: Teacher Modeling**

* **Description:** Interactive modeling sets clear, predictable expectations. 

* **Expanded Explanation:** Interactive modeling is an effective way to teach classroom expectations. When expectations are practiced, the teacher creates an efficient, predictable classroom environment where students feel safe and ready to learn. Teachers can model skills like listening, helping a classmate, or working in groups. Teachers can model routines like cleaning up, arrival/dismissal, or transitioning between classes.  Modeling starts by naming the skill and why it is important. The teacher then models the skill exactly how they want students to do it. Next, the students comment on what they observed the teacher do. The teacher will invite one or two students to model the skill exactly as they did it and the class will share what they observed again. After that, the whole class practices the skill together and the teacher provides specific feedback. 

* **Examples / Variations:** 

  * Use modeling to reteach students skills and routines after long break or disruption

  * Use modeling to clarify and reset expectations when classroom behaviors become inconsistent 

* **Reflection Prompt:** Which of your classroom skills or routines would benefit most from modeling?

* **Quiz Item:** 

  * Why is teacher modeling effective for teaching expectations? (Keywords: practice, efficient, safe, predictable)

  * What is the purpose of having the whole class practice the skill together? (Keywords: feedback, coaching, exact expectations)

* **Teacher Voice:**  “My students feel safe and ready to learn when expectations are clearly modeled and practiced.”

#### **HC_M1_STRAT4 / Strategy 4: Co-Create Classroom Rules**

* **Description:** Creating rules together builds shared responsibility. 

* **Expanded Explanation:** In a classroom where rules are co-created and consistently practiced, students are more engaged, feel safe taking risks, and develop a stronger sense of responsibility for their own actions and the well-being of the class. By involving students in the rule-making process, they can better understand the reasoning behind each rule and why it is important to follow them. Teachers can model the expected behavior for each rule for students, then ask students what they noticed about the behavior and invite a few students to model the behavior for each rule as well. Finally, they can encourage students to be observers and share how classmates followed the rules well or how they could improve.

* **Examples / Variations:** 

  * Whole-class discussion (Ask students “What rules do you think our classroom needs?” or “What rules can help us feel safe in class?”)

  * List of rules should be short and simple (Having 4-5 rules like “Be kind” or “Raise your hand” are easy to remember for students)

  * Make the rules positive (Positive phrasing like “Be a good listener” is more effective than negative phrasing like “Do not speak while others are talking)

  * Display the rules on a poster in written or picture form (The teacher and students can sign the rules to show commitment and responsibility to follow them)

  * Review the rules regularly, not only when problems arise

* **Reflection Prompt:** Why is it important for our rules to be positive?

* **Quiz Item:** Why is it important to phrase rules positively?? (Keywords: modeling, example, positive behavior)

* **Teacher Voice:** “When students help create the rules, they follow them better.”

#### **HC_M1_STRAT5 / Strategy 5: Consistent, Non-Violent Consequences**

* **Description:** Predictable consequences help children learn without fear.

* **Expanded Explanation:** Using consistent, non-violent consequences helps children understand the connection between their actions and outcomes in a safe way. When consequences are fair, clear, and linked to the unwanted behavior, students learn expectations without feeling threatened or anxious. Consistent consequences develop trust that a classroom is safe, allowing students to understand that the teacher has achievable, reasonable expectations for all learners. If a student continues to display unwanted behavior, meeting with them during individual work time to discuss the issue and set achievable goals can support the development of positive behaviors. When negative behavior persists even after consequences and an individual meeting, the teacher may choose to reach out to the student’s caregiver to address the issue collaboratively.

* **Examples / Variations:** 

  * Match the consequence to the behavior (e.g. If a student makes a mess, they clean it up).

  * Allow students space and time if they have persistent unwanted behavior. This could be having students read, draw, or take deep breaths in a separate part of the classroom. After a few minutes, or when students feel ready, they can rejoin the class and continue learning. Note that this is not a “time out” to shame or scare students, but rather an opportunity for care and peaceful reflection.

  * Apply consequences calmly and immediately after the unwanted behavior when possible. This allows the student to connect the behavior to the consequence.

  * Keep a consistent, fair process for all students.

* **Reflection Prompt:** What is a predictable consequence you could use in your classroom?

* **Quiz Item:** 

  * Why is it important for classroom consequences to be consistent? (Keywords: predictable, safe, fair, linked to behavior)

  * Choose the logical consequence for this behavior: A child wrote on his desk. (Choice: clean the desk; practice raising hand before speaking; move seats)

* **Teacher Voice:** “Being consistent helped me stop reacting emotionally.”

#### **HC_M1_STRAT6 / Strategy 6: Calm Teacher Responses**

* **Description:** Staying calm helps students regulate their emotions.

* **Expanded Explanation:** Children mirror the behavior of adults, especially in stressful situations. When teachers respond calmly, students learn to regulate their own emotions. Calm teacher responses prevent escalation, reduce anxiety, and create a safe, predictable classroom where students feel supported. That said, everyone gets frustrated sometimes and teachers may respond to students with frustration in challenging moments. Teachers can use this as an opportunity to respond to their own behaviors and model how to calmly repair by apologizing and showing empathy to impacted students. It is important to be inclusive and ensure that all students are given consistent responses. 

* **Examples / Variations:** 

  * Model mindful behaviors when feeling overwhelmed or stressed (calming breath, drinking water, stretching)

  * Model positive affirmations for students when the class/students may feel overwhelmed (“I can do hard things,” “I believe in myself and my abilities,” or “I am strong and smart.” )

  * Use reminding language to prompt students to remember classroom rules and expectations (“Think about what you can do when you finish a task early.”)

  * Use neutral body language and tone when reminding students of rules and expectations

* **Reflection Prompt:** What helps you stay calm during stressful moments?

* **Quiz Item:** What is a calming behavior you can model to students? (Keywords: deep breaths, drinking water, positive affirmations)

* **Teacher Voice:** “When I show how to calm down, students copy it.”
