### 2. Learning Objectives (2–3)

* Teachers plan active lessons using what they know about their learners.

* Teachers prepare simple adjustments so all children can participate and learn.

* Teachers use quick checks and short closing moments to guide teaching and strengthen student learning.

### 3. Teacher Motivations & Pain Points

* "My classroom is crowded, so movement and group work is difficult to organize."

* "At the end of the lesson, I run out of time and skip reviewing."

* "I try active learning, but I do not know if students are understanding the lesson."

### 4. Module Rules

* Emphasize small, realistic planning changes or additions, not complex lesson plans.
* Emphasize planning habits that support including active learning strategies into existing lessons.
* Emphasize how small planning decisions improve participation, inclusion, and student confidence.
* Normalize mistakes, asking for help, or not understanding lessons — vocalizing or showing a lack of understanding is not shameful or embarrassing.
* Emphasize the importance of creating safe rules for doing activities in small or crowded spaces.

### 5. Key Concepts

* **AIL_M6_CON1 / Planning Creates Inclusion.** Active learning is most successful when it is planned with intention. In crowded or mixed-age classrooms, small planning decisions (e.g. grouping, clear instructions, offering multiple ways to answer) can determine who participates and who is left out. Thoughtful planning helps ensure every child has a way to engage in the learning process, reducing frustration and supporting belonging.

* **AIL_M6_CON2 / Small Adjustments Make a Big Difference.** Teachers do not need to design complex, lengthy lesson plans to be inclusive. Simple changes can help more learners succeed. By planning simple options, teachers make learning more accessible without needing extra time or materials. These changes reduce stress, support confidence, and help all learners stay engaged.

* **AIL_M6_CON3 / Ongoing Observation Guides Teaching.** Teaching is not fixed once a lesson begins. By observing students and using quick checks, teachers can adjust their lesson in the moment. This is especially important in unpredictable environments where students' needs may change from day to day. In mixed-level classrooms, ongoing observation helps ensure that no learner is left behind or unchallenged. Over time, these observations help teachers recognize patterns and plan effective active and inclusive strategies that best support their learners.

* **AIL_M6_CON4 / Closure Builds Meaning and Confidence.** Ending a lesson with a clear closing moment helps children understand what they learned. In stressful or interrupted learning environments, this structure creates a sense of completion, reflection, and purpose. It also helps children feel successful and ready for the next lesson.

### 6. Strategies

#### AIL_M6_STRAT1 / Strategy 1: Know Your Learners

* **Description:** Regularly reflect on what students already know, enjoy, and find challenging.

* **Expanded Explanation:** When teachers understand their learners, they can plan activities that feel meaningful and achievable. In all classrooms, students may have different strengths and needs. When teachers have this awareness, they are able to plan intentionally to ensure all students are engaged and to prevent students from feeling left out. Teachers can choose examples and activities that connect to their students' lives, making learning feel relevant. During teaching, teachers can make observations and uncover patterns about which students participate, which students engage more in group work, which students like to show their answer with a drawing, and so on. Knowing learners takes time, and students' challenges and support needs may change throughout the school year.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Teacher keeps a list of questions to ask themselves while lesson planning to help plan for every learner (e.g. How can I offer multiple ways to respond? How can I use familiar language to explain this topic?).

  * Teacher observes which students finish independent work early and who needs extra time, making notes for tomorrow's lesson.

  * Teacher uses simple, casual conversations to learn student interests (e.g. games, daily life, songs).

  * Teacher notices which students work well together in groups and which students get off task, and may choose to pre-select groups before a lesson.

* **Reflection Prompt:** When do your students seem most engaged during the day? Why?

* **Quiz Item:** Knowing your learners helps teachers: (Options: Control behavior / Plan intentional activities / Reduce long lessons)

* **Teacher Voice:** "I realized I already observe my students, but I need to use that information when lesson planning."

#### AIL_M6_STRAT2 / Strategy 2: Use the Space Intentionally

* **Description:** Use available learning space to support safe movement and interaction.

* **Expanded Explanation:** Classrooms of all sizes can support active learning with thoughtful organization. Planning where students sit, stand, or move helps prevent confusion and accidents. Clear routines about space can make children feel secure because they know what to expect. Teachers can set routines like standing behind desks for counting or sitting on the floor mat for reading. Intentional use of space reduces stress and allows students to focus on learning instead of navigating chaos. Thinking ahead about where students will sit, stand, or gather leads to smooth transitions and reduces lost time. Keeping a classroom tidy reduces clutter and opens more space for activities. Teachers can use old cartons to store classroom materials neatly.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Students keep their backpacks on their chair or bench.

  * Teacher creates assigned group spaces around the learning environment (e.g. group 1 works by the teacher's desk, group 2 by the blackboard, group 3 on the floor mat).

  * Teacher takes students outside to do a movement activity.

  * Teacher keeps classroom materials in cartons against the wall.

  * Teacher creates a path between desks to walk around the classroom and monitor students during small group work.

  * Students and teacher pick up trash to keep the classroom space neat and clean.

  * Teacher reminds students before movement activities to create space between themselves and a neighbor.

* **Reflection Prompt:** How can you adjust your classroom space to make movement easier?

* **Quiz Item:**

  * Active learning requires a large classroom space. (Options: True / False)

  * How does organizing classroom space help reduce stress for learners? (Keywords: safe, movement, smooth transitions, predictability)

* **Teacher Voice:** "Clear spaces and routines calm my class."

#### AIL_M6_STRAT3 / Strategy 3: Plan for the Range of Learners in Your Class

* **Description:** Prepare simple adjustments so all learners can participate in a meaningful way.

* **Expanded Explanation:** In every classroom, some learners will understand quickly while others may struggle. Without planning, this can lead to boredom, frustration, or disengagement. By preparing one or two adjustments for quick learners and for below-grade-level learners, teachers can keep all students involved in lessons. During lesson planning, teachers can think about which students may need extra help and which students may need to be challenged more. Planning ahead allows for smoother, more intentional classroom learning time. Small adjustments for quick learners could include a challenge question or extension activity. Small adjustments for students who are struggling may include a simpler starting point during independent practice, peer support options, or practice questions with the teacher. These small adjustments prevent students from feeling left behind or unchallenged, creating a classroom where everyone can participate. Teachers should normalize these adjustments — the goal is not to shame or single out specific students, but to help all students grow as learners.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Teacher allows emerging writers to draw and label their idea instead of writing a complete sentence.

  * Teacher gives a challenge word problem at the end of a set of multiplication problems.

  * Teacher assigns peer support partners for students who may need extra help (e.g. quick learners support others when they finish early; struggling students can ask a peer before the teacher).

  * Teacher works with a small group of struggling learners to guide them through example questions that reflect the independent work.

  * Teacher creates a section in their lesson plan called "simple adjustments" and writes the small changes to help quick and struggling learners.

* **Reflection Prompt:** How can you challenge quick learners during your next activity?

* **Quiz Item:** Why is planning small adjustments important in mixed-age classrooms? (Keywords: inclusion, different needs, participation)

* **Teacher Voice:** "Planning simple adjustments helps me feel more prepared before I start teaching."

#### AIL_M6_STRAT4 / Strategy 4: Use Quick Checks

* **Description:** Use short, quick checks to see what students understand.

* **Expanded Explanation:** When teachers are busy teaching, it is easy to continue on without realizing that some students are confused or lost. Quick checks provide a fast, low-pressure way to understand what students are thinking without stopping the lesson or administering a formal assessment. Simple, non-verbal signals allow learners to safely show their level of understanding. Quick checks are not meant to shame or embarrass students. Teachers can model that they also do not always understand topics the first time they learn them. When teachers use quick checks, they can adjust the lesson by re-explaining, slowing down, moving on, or adding a challenge. Teachers can write quick checks into their lesson plans to help build the habit of stopping to check for understanding. During active lessons when students may be moving, working in groups, or being creative, quick checks also help teachers gauge how much time is needed before closing a lesson.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Teacher uses a question toss to see if students remember yesterday's text (e.g. "Who was the main character? What was the problem?"). If students do not remember, the teacher summarizes; if they do, the teacher moves on.

  * Teacher asks students to show a thumbs up or thumbs down if they would like to see another example during a lesson.

  * Teacher asks students to show on a scale of 1 to 5 fingers how confident they feel after a lesson (e.g. 1 is "not very confident — I want more practice" and 5 is "very confident — I am ready for the next topic") and uses the information to plan the next lesson.

  * Teacher asks students to draw an example of the new vocabulary word to show understanding.

* **Reflection Prompt:** What quick check can you try in your next lesson?

* **Quiz Item:** Why are quick checks useful during a lesson? (Keywords: immediate feedback, adjust teaching, understanding)

* **Teacher Voice:** "I can quickly see who understands without stopping everything."

#### AIL_M6_STRAT5 / Strategy 5: Close the Loop

* **Description:** End every lesson with a short moment to summarize learning.

* **Expanded Explanation:** When teachers end a lesson with a short closing moment, it helps students connect the activity to the learning goal and gives the lesson a clear purpose. This short moment can be a quick reflection, summary, challenge question, or a question for students to answer. Teachers can use this time to emphasize key ideas from a lesson, supporting students to remember what they learned. Closing each lesson with a whole-class moment is also a predictable routine that allows students to feel a sense of completion and success.

* **Examples / Variations:**

  * Teacher asks students to share what they learned from the lesson (e.g. "Who can share what we learned today?" or "Who can share what your small group worked on?").

  * Teacher provides a quick summary of the lesson and restates the learning goal.

  * Teacher asks students to share how they could use this lesson outside of the classroom (e.g. "How could you use sorting skills at home?" or "When might you need to use estimation outside of school?").

  * After summarizing the lesson, the teacher gives a challenge for students to consider (e.g. "In our story, we learned that helping others is important. Think about how you could help someone today!").

* **Reflection Prompt:** What is a closing question you can ask at the end of your next lesson?

* **Quiz Item:** What is the purpose of closing the lesson? (Options: To finish quickly / To summarize learning / To start a new topic)

* **Teacher Voice:** "Ending the lesson as a whole class creates a sense of completion."
