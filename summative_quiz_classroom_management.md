### Q3–Q4 Bank: Understanding Questions (Q3 and Q4 are UNDERSTANDING — not Application)

* Teachers can model calm and positive language in the classroom. What is one example of a positive affirmation a teacher could model and why could it help students?
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: examples like "I can keep trying even when it is hard," "Mistakes help us learn," or "I am focused," encouraging effort, building confidence, helping students feel safe
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): model, affirmation, effort, confidence, safe, calm, positive, try*

* Why can displaying a learning goal in words or pictures help students during learning activities?
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: helping students know what they are learning, supporting students with different language or reading levels, understanding the purpose of the lesson, supporting on-task learning and focus
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): goal, purpose, focus, understand, support, visual, language, on-task*

* How can classroom jobs support a positive classroom environment?
  * Acceptable answers may reflect: building responsibility, participation, belonging, leadership, shared ownership of classroom
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): responsibility, belonging, participation, ownership, contribute, leadership, community*

### Q5–Q6 Bank: Application Questions (Q5 and Q6 are BOTH APPLICATION — not Understanding)

* A student accidentally knocks over the box of chalk. Some of the chalk breaks and pieces scatter across the floor. Based on what you learned about logical consequences, what would you do next, and why?
  * Evaluated on: identifying a calm and respectful response, using the logical consequence connected to the action (clean up the spilled chalk), avoiding punishment or shaming, explaining why this response supports responsibility and keeps student dignity
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): calm, consequence, logical, clean up, dignity, responsibility, respect, shame*

* You notice that many students have started to call out answers without waiting their turn. This makes it difficult for everyone to participate in the learning process. How would you create and support a weekly class goal around this problem?
  * Evaluated on: identifying a realistic class goal, describing how the teacher would support students with the goal (e.g. reminding students at the beginning of a lesson to raise their hand), encouraging students for effort over shaming, explaining how the goal supports more students to participate
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): goal, reminder, raise hand, participation, encourage, effort, together, support*

* While reading a story, you reach a part where the main character faces a difficult problem. Instead of asking one child to share what they think will happen next, you want all students to answer. What would you do and why?
  * Evaluated on: identifying turn-and-talk, structured group work, or whole-class response strategies, connecting discussion to comprehension, encouraging students to share their thoughts to increase engagement and participation
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): turn-and-talk, partner, group, whole class, respond, share, all students, discuss, engage*

### Q8: Best Practice Question (Q8 is BEST PRACTICE — not Application, not Recall, not Understanding)

## SCORING RUBRICS

Use these rubrics to evaluate responses throughout the full quiz and the shortened retry.

**Q1–Q2 — Recall:** These questions have one correct answer each. Evaluate whether the teacher selected or stated it correctly.

**Q3–Q4 — Understanding (Short Answer):** Evaluate whether the teacher understands a concept from the course — not whether they can recite it. Do not penalize informal or simple language. Focus on whether the response reflects genuine understanding of why or how something works.

**Q5–Q6 — Application (Scenario):** Evaluate whether the teacher can apply and synthesize course concepts to a real classroom situation. Do not penalize informal language or brief responses. Focus on whether the response shows instructional reasoning applied to a realistic teaching scenario.

**Q7 — Observation (Art-Based):** The bot presents an image of a classroom interaction, lesson moment, or teacher-student exchange. The teacher identifies the strategy or strategies they observe and explains what purpose they serve. There is no single correct answer — responses must connect to course content. Evaluate: can the teacher recognize course strategies in practice when they see them?

**Q8 — Best Practice Sharing:** The bot presents a short scenario in which a fictionalized colleague is facing a challenge related to course content and asks the teacher for advice. The teacher responds as they would to a real colleague. Evaluate whether the advice is grounded in course content, appropriate to the colleague's situation, and clearly explained. Evaluate: can the teacher communicate and apply course learning in a peer or mentoring context?

### Review & Choice

- **Format:** Deliver a warm review (~10 minutes) generated by the bot based on the specific questions the teacher got wrong. Content drawn from module files. Follows `steady_path` delivery. - **Wait for acknowledgment:** After the review, wait for the teacher to acknowledge it (any affirmative reply — "okay," "ready," "got it") before offering next steps.

- **Framing:** Warm and forward-looking, never a punishment — e.g. "Let's go over a few things together, then you can pick whichever path feels right."
- **No limit on cycles:** If the teacher chooses a new quiz and scores 0–4 again, repeat this same Review & Choice flow.
