### 2. Learning Objectives (2–3)

* Teachers differentiate reading instruction so every child can participate in a meaningful way.
* Teachers use simple, quick checks to understand student learning during lessons.
* Teachers create flexible routines that reduce language barriers and build confidence.

### 3. Module Rules

* Prioritize participation not accuracy
* Multilingualism is an asset not a problem
* No focus group should be labeled as "fast" or "slow"

### 4. Key Concepts

* **READING_M6_CON1 / Access comes before achievement**. Children can not succeed in reading if they cannot access instruction. Access means understanding directions, seeing clear models, and having multiple choices to interact with content. Not all students learn in the same way, so content should be presented in different variations to allow all students to participate. When students can access instruction, they feel capable and develop learning skills. Using pictures and familiar languages can help multilingual learners access information needed to understand.

* **READING_M6_CON2 / Quick assessments guide instruction**. Teachers cannot support students if they do not know they are struggling. Quick, low-pressure checks, or formative assessments, help teachers identify students who may not understand a topic yet. Without assessment, teachers may continue with a lesson without knowing that some students are struggling. Assessing student understanding while teaching allows teachers to review, reexplain, and readjust lessons to ensure that all students are being supported.

* **READING_M6_CON3 / Language is a bridge, not a barrier**. For many children, the language they learn in at school is different from the language they speak at home. When teachers connect new vocabulary to familiar languages, comprehension skills are strengthened. Honoring home language in the classroom allows students to feel belonging and confidence. It also encourages students to remember they are capable learners, as learning in a different language can be difficult.

* **READING_M6_CON4 / Learning is stronger together**. Peers are powerful teachers. When students explain ideas to one another, both students are able to deepen their own understanding. In large classes, peer support can increase participation without requiring extra resources or materials. Collaboration allows students to view one another as co-contributors to the classroom. When peer support is a classroom expectation, students will begin to seek help and clarification from peers, not exclusively the teacher.

#### READING_M6_STRAT1 / Strategy 1: Teach Skills in Different Ways

📷 Send this image first, then deliver strategy content:
![](https://i.imgur.com/rA2k78Q.jpeg)

* **Description:** Present new reading skills by showing, saying, acting, and drawing.
* **Expanded Explanation:** Children learn through different pathways, like seeing, hearing, or doing. Teachers can incorporate visual, verbal, and physical models when teaching reading skills to make learning more accessible to all students. Using multiple pathways or approaches also helps learners to build confidence, recognizing that everyone learns in different ways and speeds. If students are confused during lessons, teachers can present another pathway to help students understand (e.g. "We practiced chopping the sounds /f//l//a//g/ flag. Now I want us to write the word and tap the sounds we hear"), ensuring that no approach is presented as better than the other. Teachers should encourage students to share the approach that helped them understand and explain their reasoning (e.g. "Did drawing a 'bowl' or acting like we were eating out of a bowl help you understand the new word more?"). Sharing can allow other students to learn from peers who may think differently from them.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher acts out vocabulary words (e.g. jump, whisper, cook)
  * When introducing a new word, teacher writes the word, clap the syllables, and draw a picture (e.g. watermelon, "wa" *clap* "ter" *clap* "mel" *clap* "on" *clap*, draw watermelon)
  * Teacher allows students to answer questions using words, drawing, or actions (e.g. "What did our character feel when they realized their jacket at school? Tell me with words, drawing or actions…. Yes, Modou is pretending to cry and Ebrima wrote sad on his slate.")
  * Teacher writes a sentence and draws a picture to explain the meaning
* **Reflection Prompt:** How do I use different teaching pathways to teach my students a new skill?
* **Teacher Voice:** "I realized some students needed to see what I was teaching, not just hear it."

#### READING_M6_STRAT2 / Strategy 2: Use Quick Checks

📷 Send this image first, then deliver strategy content:
![](https://i.imgur.com/QvThp3N.jpeg)

* **Description:** Use fast, simple gestures or tasks to see who understands.
* **Expanded Explanation:** Quick checks allow teachers to adjust instruction immediately. When introducing new concepts, a quick check, "show me" gesture, allows teachers to visually glance around the classroom to assess if their students are understanding or not. If the teacher assesses their class is understanding, they may move on with learning. However, if the teacher assesses that the class is not understanding, it allows them to address it immediately. Teachers can try explaining the content through different approaches (e.g. drawing, actions, writing, new explanation) then use another quick check to reassess student learning. Quick checks help teachers stay active in lessons and notice learning patterns of students (e.g. "My students always understand a word better when I write it and draw it."). Quick checks should be part of the learning process, not disruptive, and should not single out or shame individual students. Students should be encouraged to respond honestly, as classroom expectations have normalized making mistakes.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * "Show me with your fingers how many syllables you hear in 'airplane.'"
  * "Show me on your slate what the setting of the story is."
  * "Show me a thumbs up if you are ready to move on or a thumbs down if you want another example."
  * "Point to the picture that matches the word."
* **Reflection Prompt:** How often do I check understanding during a lesson?
* **Teacher Voice:** "I realized most of the class wanted more practice even though I thought they were ready to move on."

#### READING_M6_STRAT3 / Strategy 3: Focus Groups

📷 Send this image first, then deliver strategy content:
![](https://i.imgur.com/ANKeTZq.jpeg)

* **Description:** Grouping same-level learners to provide specific support
* **Expanded Explanation:** Focus groups allow teachers to adjust instruction based on students' current skill levels. Instead of delivering the same support to everyone, the teacher intentionally groups learners who need similar reading practice (e.g. letter-sound recognition, blending, fluency, vocabulary, or comprehension). These groups are flexible and change as students grow and develop reading skills. These are not permanent groups and should not be labeled as "fast" or "slow." Teachers can simply call names of students they want to work with on a given day. Focus groups are short and focused (10-15 minutes). The group is small (3 to 6 students) and skill-aligned. For multilingual learners, focus groups can provide additional oral language practice and vocabulary support. These groups allow students to have more chances to practice, respond, and receive specific feedback from the teacher. This builds students' confidence and reduces frustration. Focus groups are adaptable to fit the needs of a class. Teachers may place every student in a focus group or choose to only work with students who need extra support.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher uses quick checks to identify which students can be grouped for a focus group
  * During each reading lesson, the teacher works with a focus group for specific practice on that day's content while other students are doing independent work
  * Teacher places each student in a focus group and meets with a different group each day of week while other students are doing independent work
  * Teacher uses focus groups to help same-level students practice a specific letter sound they are struggling to use during blending and segmenting
  * A focus group practices the weekly text study with teacher guidance
  * A group of high-level students read a new text and answer comprehension questions
  * Multilingual learners preview new vocabulary in their focus group
* **Reflection Prompt:** How can focus groups help me respond more to support students' specific needs?
* **Teacher Voice:** "I noticed students felt safer asking questions when we worked in focus groups."

#### READING_M6_STRAT4 / Strategy 4: Support Language Use

* **Description:** Teach vocabulary with gestures, objects, sentence frames, and home-language support to strengthen understanding
* **Expanded Explanation:** Vocabulary can become more clear when it is connected to pictures, real objects, or actions. For multilingual learners, the more varied interactions with new words, the more likely they are to understand and apply it to spoken language and reading. Integrating familiar language moments during the school day allows students to collect and organize their thoughts in their home language, while sharing or discussing in the language of instruction. Teachers who consistently support language, model to students that multilingualism is an asset. Structured language supports student comprehension over time as they create a safe environment for students to interact (read, speak, write) with the school language.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher displays sentence frames in the classroom to help students structure their thoughts (e.g. "I think……because……..", "My partner said……. I agree/disagree because……")
  * Class sings a song in familiar language and then in school language
  * Class uses gestures to act out vocabulary words (e.g. shoe- pretend to put on shoes, smile- show a big smile)
  * Students create picture cards for new vocabulary words
  * Teacher allows partner or small group sharing to be done in a familiar language, or a combination of familiar and school language
* **Reflection Prompt:** How do I treat home languages as strengths?
* **Teacher Voice:** "Students answered with more confidence when they shared ideas with a partner in their home language first."

#### READING_M6_STRAT5 / Strategy 5: Use Peer Support

* **Description:** Use pairs and small groups let children explain, demonstrate, and encourage one another
* **Expanded Explanation:** Students can learn not only from the teacher, but also one another. Peers can assist learning by explaining their thought process or ideas. Peer support helps strengthen understanding for both students, as explaining content reemphasizes key concepts. Supportive peer interactions encourage students to ask each other questions to clarify instructions or content, instead of exclusively relying on the teacher. In crowded classrooms, peer support builds belonging and increases engagement without needing extra materials. Teachers utilize peer support to increase classroom participation for struggling learners and multilingual learners. Teachers should model how to help classmates using respectful language and monitor peer support conversations.
* **Examples / Variations:**
  * Teacher uses turn-and-talk after a question (e.g. "Can you brainstorm rhyming words? Turn to a partner and then we will share our words as a whole class.")
  * Group retells a story in sequence (e.g. "Work together in your group to retell the story. Each person should participate in the retelling.")
  * Teacher sits strong readers and emerging readers around each other to make peer support accessible
  * "Ask 3 before me," students ask three different peers a question before they can ask the teacher
  * Teacher displays sentence starters to frame supportive peer language ("This is my thinking…" "I am confused, can you help me…?")
* **Reflection Prompt:** How do my students support each other respectfully?
* **Teacher Voice:** "Students began asking peers for support instead of only asking me."
