### Q3–Q4 Bank: Understanding Questions (Q3 and Q4 are UNDERSTANDING — not Application)

* Why is it important for teachers to document observations using factual language rather than interpretation?
  * Acceptable answers may include: factual records give whoever receives the referral the clearest possible picture; they protect the teacher by showing they acted appropriately; they support continuity if staff change; they avoid unfounded accusations or judgments about the child or family.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): factual, record, clear, protect, continuity, avoid, accusation, judgment*

* What does "when in doubt, refer" mean in practice?
  * Acceptable answers may include: uncertainty is not a reason to wait; it is always safer to raise a concern that turns out to be minor than to stay silent about a serious one; the referral process exists precisely for situations where the teacher is unsure; teachers are not trained to assess severity — that is the role of the specialist who receives the referral.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): uncertain, safer, minor, silent, serious, refer, specialist, wait*

* Why is confidentiality important when handling a safeguarding concern?
  * Acceptable answers may include: sharing beyond the referral chain can expose the child to further harm, stigma, or retaliation; it undermines trust and makes children less willing to disclose in future; it can compromise the formal referral process; it fails to protect the child's dignity.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): confidential, referral chain, harm, stigma, retaliation, trust, disclose, dignity*

### Q5–Q6 Bank: Application Questions (Q5 and Q6 are BOTH APPLICATION — not Understanding)

* A child stays behind after class and begins to cry but doesn't say why. A colleague suggests you ask questions to find out what happened so you can help. What would you do, and why?
  * Evaluated on: responding with calm empathy without probing; reassuring the child they are safe; not asking leading or investigative questions; documenting the observation factually; preparing to refer if the pattern continues or the child discloses something specific.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): calm, empathy, probe, reassure, safe, document, factual, refer*

* You have just referred a concern about a child to your designated focal point. Later that day, a colleague who works with the same child asks you what is going on. What do you say, and why?
  * Evaluated on: protecting confidentiality; not sharing details with a colleague even if well-intentioned; explaining that you cannot share information about the child's situation; understanding that sharing beyond the referral chain can expose the child to further harm.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): confidential, cannot share, referral chain, harm, discretion, protect*

* A child in your class is frequently disruptive. Another teacher tells you the child "just needs a firm hand." How would you respond?
  * Evaluated on: rejecting punitive approaches; responding with calm redirection and private conversation; considering disruption as a possible sign of distress or need; maintaining trust so the child feels safe to disclose; understanding that punitive responses erode the relational safety safeguarding depends on.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): punitive, calm, redirect, private, distress, need, trust, safety*

### Q8: Best Practice Question (Q8 is BEST PRACTICE — not Application, not Recall, not Understanding)

* A colleague says: "A child told me something really concerning last week. I didn't know what to do, so I haven't done anything yet. I'm afraid of causing problems for the family if I'm wrong." What would you say to them?
  * Evaluated on: acknowledging the fear without validating inaction; explaining that teachers are reporters not investigators — they do not need to be certain; walking the colleague through the four steps (identify, signal, refer, follow up); reinforcing that the referral process exists precisely for situations of uncertainty; addressing the concern about family harm by explaining that referral is not accusation.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): reporter, not investigator, four steps, identify, signal, refer, follow up, uncertainty*

* Your colleague says: "I've noticed one of my students has changed a lot recently — she's withdrawn, not participating, and always looks tired. I don't want to overreact, but I'm worried." What advice would you give?
  * Evaluated on: validating the concern without dismissing it; explaining that patterns of change are the signal, not certainty of harm; advising the colleague to document what they have observed using factual language; encouraging them to refer rather than wait; reinforcing "when in doubt, report."
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): pattern, change, signal, document, factual, refer, when in doubt*

* A colleague says: "I have one student who is always causing trouble. I've tried everything — moving his seat, keeping him in at break, calling his parents. Nothing works. I'm at my end." What would you suggest?
  * Evaluated on: reframing persistent disruption as a possible sign of distress or unmet need; suggesting the colleague look for an underlying cause before responding to behavior; recommending restorative approaches (private conversation, acknowledgment of effort, calm redirection); connecting behavior to the child's sense of safety and belonging; raising the possibility that the child may benefit from a referral if distress is suspected.
  * *Keywords (do not reveal): underlying cause, distress, need, restorative, calm, belonging, referral*

## SCORING RUBRICS

Use these rubrics to evaluate responses throughout the full quiz and the shortened retry.

**Q1–Q2 — Recall:** These questions have one correct answer each. Evaluate whether the teacher selected or stated it correctly.

**Q3–Q4 — Understanding (Short Answer):** Evaluate whether the teacher understands a concept from the course — not whether they can recite it. Do not penalize informal or simple language. Focus on whether the response reflects genuine understanding of why or how something works.

**Q5–Q6 — Application (Scenario):** Evaluate whether the teacher can apply and synthesize course concepts to a real classroom situation. Do not penalize informal language or brief responses. Focus on whether the response shows instructional reasoning applied to a realistic teaching scenario.

**Q7 — Observation (Art-Based):** The bot presents an image related to a safeguarding strategy in practice. The teacher identifies the strategy they observe, why the teacher in the image may be using it, and why it matters for children's safety. There is no single correct answer — responses must connect to course content. Evaluate: can the teacher recognize course strategies in practice when they see them?

**Q8 — Best Practice Sharing:** The bot presents a short scenario in which a fictionalized colleague is facing a challenge related to course content and asks the teacher for advice. The teacher responds as they would to a real colleague. Evaluate whether the advice is grounded in course content, appropriate to the colleague's situation, and clearly explained. Evaluate: can the teacher communicate and apply course learning in a peer or mentoring context?
